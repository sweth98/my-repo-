HydroAura — making a daily habit visible, social and easy to read at a glance.

A hydration and health-tracking app designed across four primary screens — Today, History, Social and Settings — built around data visualization and habit-formation rather than a single "log water" form.

01 — The problem

Hydration tracking is a habit app first, and a data app second.

Most hydration trackers reduce to a single number — "how much water today" — which is easy to design but hard to stay motivated by on its own. HydroAura needed to make daily intake understandable at a glance (am I on track right now?), show progress over time (is this actually becoming a habit?), and add a reason to keep coming back beyond the number itself.

The interface targets a general, health-conscious mobile user who wants a quick daily check-in rather than a detailed health-data dashboard — someone more likely to open the app for ten seconds several times a day than for one long session.

02 — Design goals

What the interface needed to accomplish

  • Make "am I on track" answerable in one glance. A large circular progress indicator on the Today screen shows current intake against goal without requiring any reading.
  • Turn history into a pattern, not a log. A weekly bar chart with a temperature overlay on the History screen, rather than a plain list of past entries.
  • Add a social layer without adding pressure. A Social/Circle tab shows friends' progress as supportive context, not a leaderboard.
  • Let the user tune how the app nags them. Granular reminder and quiet-hours controls in Settings, so notifications support the habit instead of becoming a reason to disable them.

05 — Design decisions

Why the interface looks and behaves the way it does

Typography

What: Large numerals for the hero metrics (intake volume, completion percentage) with clean, restrained labels underneath.

Why: The core question on every screen is a number — "how much," "what percent," "which day." Making the number the loudest element on the screen, rather than the label around it, respects how quickly the app is meant to be read.

Color

What: A cool blue as the dominant color (matching the water/hydration theme), with a warm accent used for streaks and weather-linked prompts, and neutral surface cards for structure.

Why: Blue is a literal and emotional match for a hydration product — calm, clean, associated with water — while reserving a warmer accent for streaks and achievements keeps positive-reinforcement moments visually distinct from routine data.

Layout

What: A consistent card-grid structure across all four tabs — stat cards, a hero metric, then a list/chart — with the same bottom tab bar throughout.

Why: Keeping the same structural rhythm on every screen (hero stat → supporting cards → detail list) means a returning user doesn't have to relearn the layout each time they switch tabs; only the data changes.

Components

What: Circular progress ring, weekly bar chart with a temperature line overlay, quick-add intake buttons (250ml / 500ml / custom), friend progress cards with mini progress bars, and a granular reminder-frequency slider.

Why: Quick-add buttons cover the most common intake amounts in one tap, so logging never requires opening a form. The friend cards reuse the same progress-bar pattern as the personal dashboard, so the social layer reads as an extension of the same system rather than a separate feature.

Interaction design

What: Contextual prompt cards (e.g. a "Morning Boost" suggestion, weather-linked reminders) that surface a suggested action rather than a generic notification.

Why: A suggestion tied to time of day or weather feels like guidance rather than a generic push notification, which supports the habit-formation goal without adding pressure.

Potential improvement

The exact feedback moment when a daily goal is hit isn't shown in the static screens — a small celebratory state on the Today screen (beyond the notification card) would reinforce the habit loop more directly.

07 — Accessibility

Considerations built into the screens

Progress and stats are shown as both a visual (ring, bar) and a number (ml, %, day count), so the information doesn't rely on color or shape alone. Quick-add buttons and toggle controls are sized for one-handed mobile use. Layouts were checked against WCAG 2.1 contrast guidance and tested for responsive behaviour across device sizes.

08 — What I learned

Reflection

  • Designing four tabs that all needed to feel like "the same app" pushed me to build a repeatable card/hero-stat pattern early, rather than designing each screen independently and reconciling them afterward.
  • The social/circle feature was the hardest to get right — the line between "motivating" and "comparison pressure" is a design decision, not just a data decision, and it shows up in small choices like showing progress bars instead of ranked positions.
  • Next time, I'd design the goal-achieved moment explicitly as its own state, since habit apps live or die on how rewarding that specific moment feels.
