# Newton's Three Laws of Motion - Script and Storyboard

Audience: Class 9-10 (CBSE/ICSE). Target: 1920x1080, 30 fps, about 4-5 minutes.

## Hook  (38 words, about 15s of speech)

**h1** [question] "Why do you lurch forward when a bus suddenly brakes?"  
> Visual: Local bus drives across a Mumbai-style street. Mascot Newt (little robot student) stands inside holding a pole. Bus screeches, Newt squashes and stretches forward. Freeze-frame with a big question mark.

**h2** [surprise] "Nobody pushed you. So what just happened?"  
> Visual: Rewind effect, slow-motion replay, arrows ask 'who pushed?' and fade to nothing.

**h3** [fun] "The answer hides in three rules from Sir Isaac Newton."  
> Visual: Whip-pan to a navy night sky. Three glowing cards (blue, orange, green) pop in with an apple that wobbles.

**h4** [fun] "Let's master them with autos, cricket balls and an ISRO rocket!"  
> Visual: Auto-rickshaw, cricket ball and rocket zoom across the screen, title 'Newton's Three Laws' slams in with screen shake.

## Law 1 - Inertia  (161 words, about 62s of speech)

**a1** [fun] "Law number one. The law of inertia."  
> Visual: Blue shape-wipe transition. Chapter tag 'LAW 1' appears top-left. Title with bounce.

**a2** [definition] "An object stays at rest, or keeps moving in a straight line at constant speed, unless an unbalanced force acts on it."  
> Visual: Kinetic typography: key words (rest, straight line, constant speed, unbalanced force) pop in time with the voice and glow blue.

**a3** [explain] "Balanced forces cancel, like a tug of war between equal teams. Unbalanced forces change the motion."  
> Visual: Tug of war: two equal force arrows (50 N each) keep the knot still. Then one arrow grows to 80 N, arrow turns red and the knot slides.

**a4** [fun] "Back to the bus. Your body was moving with it. The bus stops, but your body keeps going. So you lurch forward!"  
> Visual: Split replay of the bus. Dotted line shows the bus stopping while Newt's path continues. Speed lines, dust puff, screen shake.

**a5** [definition] "That is inertia: the tendency to resist any change in motion."  
> Visual: The word INERTIA is built letter by letter with overshoot.

**a6** [fun] "Inertia of rest. Yank a tablecloth fast and the plates stay put. Flick the card and the coin drops into the glass. Beat a dusty carpet and the dust falls out."  
> Visual: Three mini-demos in a row, each dimmed until its sentence: tablecloth trick, coin on a card dropping into a glass, carpet beating with dust puffs.

**a7** [fun] "Inertia of motion. A runner keeps running a few steps after the finish line."  
> Visual: Runner crosses the finish tape, slides forward with squash and stretch, motion blur.

**a8** [fun] "Inertia of direction. When an auto turns left, you slide right."  
> Visual: Top-down view of an auto taking a left turn, passenger dot continues straight along a dotted arrow.

**a9** [surprise] "Heavier objects have more inertia. Mass measures inertia."  
> Visual: Split screen: light football vs heavy iron ball, same push; heavy one barely moves. Label 'More mass = more inertia'.

**a10** [explain] "That is why seatbelts matter. In a crash the car stops, but you don't. The belt stops you safely."  
> Visual: Slow-motion crash freeze frame with callout labels 'Car stops' and 'Body keeps moving'; seatbelt snaps across Newt and a green tick pops.

## Law 2 - F = ma  (201 words, about 78s of speech)

**b1** [question] "Which is harder to push: an empty cart, or a cart full of vegetables?"  
> Visual: Orange wipe. Chapter tag 'LAW 2'. Split screen: Newt pushes an empty cart (fast, light) vs a full vegetable cart (slow, heavy). Wind-up anticipation before each push.

**b2** [fun] "The full one! Now give a cricket ball and a football the same kick. The lighter cricket ball speeds up more."  
> Visual: Same-size force arrow (40 N) on both. Cricket ball (0.16 kg) flies in a big arc, football (0.43 kg) in a lower arc. Arrows and numbers labelled.

**b3** [definition] "Law number two. Acceleration is directly proportional to the net force, and inversely proportional to the mass."  
> Visual: Kinetic typography in orange. The words 'directly' and 'inversely' flip with a small bounce.

**b4** [formula] "Together, that is F equals m times a."  
> Visual: The equation assembles piece by piece: F slides in from left, = drops from top, m pops, x spins in, a zooms in. Pieces glow in turn.

**b5** [formula] "F is force in newtons. m is mass in kilograms. a is acceleration in metres per second squared."  
> Visual: Each letter gets a unit tag: N, kg, m/s². Tags land with a pop.

**b7** [explain] "Momentum is mass times velocity. Force is how fast momentum changes. For constant mass, that is F equals m times a."  
> Visual: Equation morphs: p = m v, then F = change in p / change in t, then F = m a. Match-cut back to the main formula.

**b8** [fun] "Double the force, double the acceleration. Double the mass, half the acceleration."  
> Visual: Live bar graph: force bar doubles, acceleration bar doubles; mass doubles, acceleration bar halves.

**b9** [fun] "Numerical time! An auto-rickshaw of five hundred kilograms gets a net force of one thousand newtons. Find its acceleration, and its speed after five seconds from rest."  
> Visual: Chalkboard card slides in with the question. Auto-rickshaw sits at the start line.

**b10** [formula] "Given: m is five hundred kilograms, F is one thousand newtons, u is zero."  
> Visual: 'Given' list types in with units; each value highlights as spoken.

**b12** [formula] "a equals F divided by m, so a equals one thousand divided by five hundred, which is two metres per second squared."  
> Visual: Numbers drop in, counter animates 1000 / 500 = 2 m/s². Auto accelerates slowly with speed lines.

**b13** [formula] "Then v equals u plus a times t, which is zero plus two times five, so ten metres per second."  
> Visual: Live speedometer and velocity-time graph draw a straight line up to 10 m/s at t = 5 s.

**b14** [surprise] "That is thirty six kilometres per hour!"  
> Visual: Speedometer needle overshoots and settles at 36 km/h; confetti sparks. Newt cheers.

## Law 3 - Action and Reaction  (134 words, about 52s of speech)

**c1** [fun] "Law number three! Action and reaction."  
> Visual: Green wipe. Chapter tag 'LAW 3'. Two hands push each other and spring apart.

**c2** [definition] "For every action, there is an equal and opposite reaction."  
> Visual: Kinetic typography; 'equal' and 'opposite' appear as two arrows pointing apart.

**c3** [explain] "If A pushes B, then B pushes A back, equally hard, in the opposite direction."  
> Visual: Two skaters push off each other; arrows of equal length (60 N each) in opposite directions, skaters slide apart (lighter one faster).

**c4** [fun] "An ISRO rocket pushes hot gases down, and the gases push the rocket up."  
> Visual: Rocket on pad, anticipation shake, countdown, lift-off with flame particles; gas arrow down, thrust arrow up; screen shake.

**c5** [fun] "A swimmer pushes water back, and the water pushes the swimmer forward."  
> Visual: Side view of a swimmer in a pool, water arrows backward and forward.

**c6** [fun] "A gun pushes the bullet forward, and the bullet kicks the gun back. That is recoil."  
> Visual: Toy cannon on a cart fires a ball; cannon rolls back with a puff of smoke. Labels 'Action' and 'Reaction'.

**c7** [fun] "When you walk, your foot pushes the ground back, and the ground pushes you forward."  
> Visual: Foot-level view; arrow on ground and arrow on foot; Newt slips on a banana peel with squash and stretch.

**c8** [fun] "A released balloon: air rushes out, and the balloon zooms the other way!"  
> Visual: Balloon released, flies around in a loopy arc with a wobbling tail.

**c9** [question] "But if the forces are equal and opposite, why don't they cancel?"  
> Visual: Freeze frame, question marks orbit Newt's head.

**c10** [explain] "Because they act on different objects! Action on the ball, reaction on your foot. Forces cancel only on the same object."  
> Visual: Foot and ball, two coloured arrows each with a halo around its own object; a dotted line shows 'cancel' only works inside one halo.

## Recap and Quiz  (120 words, about 46s of speech)

**r1** [upbeat] "Recap! Law one: objects resist changes in motion."  
> Visual: Three cards fly in, blue card flips up first with an inertia icon.

**r2** [upbeat] "Law two: force equals mass times acceleration."  
> Visual: Orange card flips, F = m a pulses.

**r3** [upbeat] "Law three: every action has an equal and opposite reaction."  
> Visual: Green card flips, two arrows bounce apart.

**r4** [upbeat] "Quick quiz! Three questions, five seconds each."  
> Visual: Newt in a game-show pose, 'QUIZ TIME' banner with confetti.

**q1** [upbeat] "Question one. A bus stops and you lurch forward. A, law one? B, law two? C, law three?"  
> Visual: Options A, B, C slide in; 5 second ring countdown timer.

**q1a** [upbeat] "A! Law one."  
> Visual: Wrong options fade, A grows green with a tick; Newt jumps.

**q2** [upbeat] "Question two. Twenty newtons acts on a four kilogram trolley. What is its acceleration in metres per second squared? A, two. B, five. C, eighty."  
> Visual: Trolley, options A, B, C, 5 second countdown.

**q2a** [upbeat] "B! Twenty divided by four is five."  
> Visual: B glows; a = 20 / 4 = 5 m/s² counts up.

**q3** [upbeat] "Question three. A rocket rises because gases are pushed down. A, law one? B, law two? C, law three?"  
> Visual: Rocket icon, options A, B, C, 5 second countdown.

**q3a** [upbeat] "C! Action and reaction."  
> Visual: C glows green; rocket lifts off.

**r5** [upbeat] "You nailed it! Go spot Newton's laws everywhere. See you next time!"  
> Visual: Newt waves, three cards orbit, end card with fade-out.
