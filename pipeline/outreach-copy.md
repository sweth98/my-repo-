### Post 2: Data-sovereignty-with-AI insight
> A question I hear more often now: "We want AI in our courses, but where does our learner data go?"
>
> Most AI features in learning tools work by sending content to a third-party model. For many institutions and regulated companies, that raises real questions: who can access it, where it is processed, and whether it fits GDPR, FERPA or India's DPDP expectations.
>
> There is another route: run the platform and the AI inside your own network or private cloud. You keep control of the data, the logs and the access rules. The trade-off is that someone has to deploy and maintain it, which is why a managed pilot is a sensible first step.
>
> How is your institution handling this today? I'd like to hear what's working.
>
> #AI #DataPrivacy #EdTech #HigherEd #LearningAndDevelopment

### Post 3: How-to
> How to evaluate any AI feature in a learning platform, in five questions:
>
> 1. Where is the content processed, and by whom?
> 2. Is learner data used to train any model? Can you get that in writing?
> 3. Can the AI run inside your own network or private cloud?
> 4. What does your audit trail show: who prompted what, and when?
> 5. What happens to your data and models if you leave the vendor?
>
> If you can't get clear answers to all five, run a small pilot with one department before committing campus-wide.
>
> I work on private-deployment learning platforms, so I'm biased toward question 3, but I'd use this checklist whoever the vendor is. What would you add?
>
> #LMS #AI #Procurement #EdTech
