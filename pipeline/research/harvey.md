## 6. Pilot-to-contract conversion
No published conversion rate. The evidence is anecdotal: A&O (trial to firmwide), Macfarlanes (70 to about 950), Honigman (10% to about 35%). Treat these as selected successes.

## 7. Distinctive tactics (supported by sources)
1. Prestige first customer, with a public announcement (A&O). The customer's own press release did the credibility work.
2. Exclusivity framing (A&O "exclusive launch partnership"; PwC reported as exclusive among the Big Four).
3. Scarcity through early access, which lasted until 2024.
4. Certifications stacked early and often, with a public trust center and a security addendum.
5. Channel partners that already hold client trust (PwC).
6. Pilots sized to prove usage (70 lawyers, 3,500 lawyers), then seat expansion.

# Lessons for a small B2B vendor selling a private on-prem LMS with in-network AI to universities and corporate L&D
1. Your privacy architecture is a stronger claim than Harvey's. Harvey relies on vendor assertions (zero retention at model providers), and I found no independent verification. Data that never leaves the customer's network is verifiable by the buyer. Lead with that, and document it in a short architecture diagram for security reviewers.
2. Get certifications and a trust page before scale. Harvey publishes SOC 2 Type II, ISO 27001 and a security addendum. A small vendor can start with a security questionnaire pack, a SOC 2 or ISO 27001 roadmap with dates, and a standard data-processing agreement. Do not claim certifications you do not hold.
3. Win one named, respected reference through a scoped pilot. A&O's trial (3,500 users) became a public partnership. Offer one university or one corporate L&D team a time-boxed pilot with success metrics defined up front, and negotiate permission to name them. Expect this to be slow, and do not assume it will convert. Harvey publishes no conversion rate.
4. Plan the land-and-expand path from the start. Macfarlanes went from 70 users to about 950. Price per seat or per cohort, track usage (Harvey cites 77% seat utilization), and show the champion a usage report to support the expansion ask.
5. Use trusted channels and be honest about limits. Harvey leveraged PwC and its law-firm customers to reach corporates. Look for LMS integrators, consortia, and L&D consultancies. Founder-led outreach worked for Harvey early (thousands of LinkedIn messages), but it was backed by a product people wanted to try. The lessons are partly survivorship-biased, given Harvey's venture funding and OpenAI relationship. A small vendor should expect a longer cycle.
